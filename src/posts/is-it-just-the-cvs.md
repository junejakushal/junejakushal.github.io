At Cornell, after the war, Feynman was offered the kind of job most academics dream about: a position at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. No teaching. No classes. Nothing to do but think.

He did not want it. He had watched brilliant people go there and stall. With no students and no classes, there was nothing to keep them going on the days the ideas did not come. His own view, as he puts it in *Surely You're Joking*:

> I don't believe I can really do without teaching. The reason is, I have to have something so that when I don't have any ideas and I'm not getting anywhere I can say to myself, "At least I'm living; at least I'm doing something; I am making some contribution."

Teaching was not a cost he paid to do physics. It was part of what kept him able to do it.

Trevor Noah felt the same way about *The Daily Show* and his stand-up. Each one made the other possible.

On paper, the job is simple. XLRI's [2019 convocation report](https://acad.xlri.ac.in/xluploads/convo/2019/files/basic-html/page90.html) describes it like this:

> CRISP is a committee at XLRI that undertakes the entire process of preparation of students for their placement processes, namely the summer internship process for first-year students and the lateral and final placement processes for second-year students.

The first two activities on its list are about CVs: mapping each candidate to a senior "with similar profiles so as to increase inter-batch interaction," and "ensuring the authenticity of information provided on the CVs through a meticulous data verification process."

So on paper, you only have to make sure the CVs get made, the prep gets done, and last year's interview experiences get passed down. That is the whole description.

What the description leaves out is the committee doing it. Nineteen people. Two campuses, Jamshedpur and Delhi. Two streams of students, BM and HR. At any given moment, Delhi might be handling Jamshedpur's work, Jamshedpur might be handling Delhi's, or both might be untangling something together. CRISP is the only committee that has to work across campuses just to keep its everyday work running.

And nobody joins it for the CV. There is no fund to manage, no event with tens of thousands of footfall to put in a highlight line. Just nineteen very different people who volunteered, setting their egos and their sleep aside so that the placement machinery of a B-school keeps chugging along.

Is it just the CVs?

Then D-Day comes, and there is nothing left to do.

You get them mints before the personal interview. After it, sour candy, a Chupa Chups, anything to bring the nerves down.

It is their interview. Their group discussion. You cannot walk into the room with them, and nothing you say in the corridor changes what happens inside it.

But you still have to be there.
